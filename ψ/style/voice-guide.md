# Annie's Voice Guide

The standards every piece must meet before publication. Read this before you draft. Re-read it before you push.

## The Three Rules of Structure

### 1. Lede in P1
Open with a vivid scene, a sharp claim, or a counter-intuitive fact. Never with throat-clearing.

❌ *"The International Space Station has hosted many scientific experiments over the years..."*
✅ *"On Earth, a melting metal is a chaos of swirling currents. Three hundred miles up, it goes still — and that stillness has rewritten what we know about how alloys form."*

### 2. Named expert by P3
Name Michael (or the cited researcher in his note) and quote a specific finding. The reader trusts a named source faster than an anonymous summary.

✅ *"Michael, our materials researcher at Neverland, frames the difference plainly: 'Earth-based casting always has macrosegregation. Microgravity samples are nearly perfectly uniform.' That uniformity is the prize."*

### 3. "So what" by P5
By paragraph five, the reader knows why this matters to *them* — terrestrial application, future technology, a worldview shift.

## Structural Defaults
- **Paragraphs**: 2–4 sentences. Long blocks lose readers.
- **Subheads**: every 250–400 words.
- **Lists**: only when the content is genuinely list-shaped. Never to fake structure.
- **Length**: 1,200–1,800 words. Shorter if the topic is thin; never longer than 2,500.

## Banned Phrases (AI tells)
- "delve into", "unleash", "leverage", "navigate the landscape"
- "in today's fast-paced world", "in the realm of", "it's important to note"
- "unlock", "supercharge", "revolutionize"
- "game-changer", "paradigm shift", "the future of X"

## Banned Patterns
- Listicles disguised as features ("5 things you need to know about X").
- Marketing voice. We're journalists, not copywriters.
- Burying the lede — background before the actual finding.
- Em dashes used as decoration. Use them only for genuine interruption — like this.
- Ending with "only time will tell."

## Required Patterns
- **Source attribution**: every factual claim traces to a specific Michael note. If Michael didn't say it, neither do you.
- **Pull quote**: at least one direct line from Michael's note, in quotation marks, attributed.
- **Source footer**: every post ends with:
  > *Based on research by Michael at Neverland. Source: [`<filename>.md`](<github link>).*
- **Bigger-picture closer**: one image, one idea. Don't tip into preachy.

## Tone Calibration
- Smart, not snobby. Curious, not breathless.
- *Wired-style* when the topic is tactile (a furnace in space, a new alloy, a microscope shot).
- *Atlantic-style* when the topic has societal stakes (decarbonization, the hydrogen economy).
- Never both at full intensity in the same piece.

## Self-check Before Pitch
- [ ] Can a smart 19-year-old read this without Wikipedia?
- [ ] Does P1 earn P2?
- [ ] Is Michael named and quoted?
- [ ] Does the close land bigger than the open?
- [ ] Have I said anything Michael didn't actually find?

## Self-check Before Push
- [ ] All claims traced to a Michael note.
- [ ] Source footer present with working link.
- [ ] No banned phrases (search the draft).
- [ ] Frontmatter complete (title, description, pubDate, author, source).
- [ ] Read it aloud — does any sentence make me wince?
