# Editorial Lessons — Report #2 (iss-metallurgy)
*From Jeans review, 2026-05-09*

## What held
- Lede: "It melts quietly" — concrete, counterintuitive, entire argument in two words. **This is the standard.**
- Angle discipline: one lens (calibration source) held from P1 to close. Never drifted.
- Quotes: placed where they do argumentative work, not decoration.
- Close: end bigger than you opened. Connect science to something the reader carries out.

## Five things to sharpen (apply to every report going forward)

### 1. Hardware sections — physical image, not glossary
When listing equipment, find *one physical detail* that anchors the reader. What does the machine look like doing its job? Scale? Sound? "The metal floats" worked; "the MSL furnace heats samples to X" did not. Technical language lands only after the reader can see the thing.

### 2. Counterintuitive findings — let them breathe
When the science surprises (coarser dendrites in space, not finer), slow down *before* the explanation. Give the reader a beat to feel the surprise. The "fundamentally different solidification regime" sentence was in the piece but needed to be an *emotional* moment, not just a clause in a run of paragraphs.

### 3. No meta-commentary in section openers
"Here is where the story tends to drift, and where precision matters" — cut this. Never tell the reader what is about to happen. Open the section with the argument directly. Trust the reader.

### 4. Section headers should do work
"The Hardware" = label. "The End of the Experiment" = slightly elegiac, tells you something before you read. Aim for the second register on every header. A good header makes a small claim.

### 5. Source footnotes — real paths or nothing
Placeholder links (`github.com/Poon/Michael/...`) undermine attribution. Use actual repo-relative paths confirmed to exist, or omit hyperlinks entirely. Attribution standard is non-negotiable.

## The bar
Every lede: concrete, physical, argument-in-three-words.  
Every close: bigger than the open.  
The middle is where the next report improves.
